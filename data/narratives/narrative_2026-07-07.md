## Risk lens

**1. The 30Y at 5% and tech at record levels is a structural contradiction that resolves at Q2 earnings.** History: the last time the 30Y touched 5% was October 2023 (the "higher for longer" peak). That preceded a 20%+ S&P rally as the Fed pivoted. The current context is different: EFFR at 3.63% (already cut), Warsh "no hints" on further cuts, and the 30Y rising despite lower EFFR = the term premium is growing DESPITE low policy rates. Two scenarios:
- Tech earnings justify higher long rates → "no landing" regime, S&P to 7,600+
- Tech earnings disappoint + 30Y stays at 5% → DCF models reprice violently from the top; VIX goes from 16 to 25+

Neither outcome has materialized yet. The 30Y is the clock.

**2. The yen carry trade is at maximum reflexive vulnerability.** USD/JPY at 162.33 (40-year yen low). TSMC +4.63% on the same day. The yen-funded AI chip carry trade is directly operating: cheap yen borrowing → buy TSMC/ASML/Korean semis → FX exposure amplifies returns. The reflexivity: as positions win, capital stays on (or expands), pushing USD/JPY higher, making the trade cheaper in yen terms. This self-reinforcement has a singular reversal risk: a BoJ hike or hawkish statement mid-July unwinds all of it simultaneously. The July 6 session of TSMC +4.63% + USD/JPY +1.10 is the carry trade at its most leveraged.

**3. The AI capex ownership model is shifting, not contracting.** The market is now distinguishing between two types of AI infrastructure commitments:
- **Old model (Blackstone/QTS, July 3):** General-purpose data centers, hyperscaler-served, canceled = demand fragility signal
- **New model (TeraWulf/Anthropic, July 6):** AI-specific neocloud operators, long-term leases, $19B = demand commitment signal

For risk analysts: the capex is not declining — it's reorganizing. The EARNINGS impact differs: hyperscalers' capex was a negative for their FCF. Neocloud operators' capex is their core business model. Anthropic paying $19B in rent generates rental income for TeraWulf. This is a bullish structural shift for AI infrastructure equities (neoclouds, power, networking), potentially neutral for mega-cap hyperscalers. Morgan Stanley's "semiconductor momentum fades, new market leaders emerge" report lands in this context — the new leaders may be AI-adjacent (power, networking, neoclouds) not the semis themselves.

**4. CFTC June 30 data is a genuine binary.** Two scenarios with opposite implications:
- **Nasdaq lev_net below −60k** (bears added through the holiday): Going into earnings with a heavily shorted Nasdaq while the TeraWulf/Anthropic deal is live = classic squeeze setup if Q2 beats. A -70k+ print + in-line ASML guidance = violent short-squeeze. The July 6 +4.63% TSMC move may already be early covering.
- **Nasdaq lev_net above −30k** (bears covered): Squeeze risk is off. The Q2 earnings then play on fundamentals, not positioning. Either direction more moderate.

The July 6 TSMC/ASML +4-5% move is ambiguous: it could be (a) fundamental buyers on the Anthropic deal, (b) bears covering before CFTC data drops, or (c) algorithm-driven momentum reversal after oversold levels from the holiday de-risk.

**5. Running watch-rate accountability: 30% hit rate over 70 triggers.** Seven consecutive sessions of bear/flat stances with market-timing consistently wrong. The structural thesis (private credit stress, NFCI lag, earnings bubble, yen carry vulnerability) may be correct — these are real risks. But the market-timing of catalyst→price translation has been off by session after session. The lesson: structural risk theses require an explicit TRIGGER that the market will respond to before re-arming the directional trade. CFTC June 30 + Q2 earnings week = those triggers. Until they arrive, no edge pressing either direction.
