## Risk lens

**1. Three hike votes: the policy error tail is now priced in but not sized.**

The market's reaction to three FOMC hike dissenters was 10Y +6bps and VIX −1.93%. That combination says: bond market is already partially pricing a September hike possibility; equity market thinks it won't happen. The disconnect is the risk. If September FOMC has four dissenters (needing only one more vote), Warsh's hold becomes politically untenable and the committee moves toward consensus for a hike. A rate hike at 1.5% GDP / +57k NFP / 3.53% CPI would be the most aggressive tightening-into-slowdown call since 1980. The precedent matters: Volcker hiked in a slowdown and it worked, but it required 18 months of pain. The market is not pricing 18 months of pain.

**2. HY OAS 2.87% (57.9th %ile): the median is a regime marker, not a trigger.**

The transition from below-median to above-median credit is a regime shift, not a buy/sell signal. What changes above the median:
- Credit becomes a DRAG on the equity risk premium (when OAS is cheap, equity multiples can expand; when OAS is above median, equity multiples face a headwind from the credit side of the discount rate)
- The sequence from here to 65th–70th %ile is the "mild stress" zone where IG typically begins to follow HY
- IG OAS at 68.3th %ile (unchanged for multiple sessions) is the critical holdout. If IG starts widening from 0.81%, the credit cascade to the next quality tier begins

Six consecutive HY OAS widening prints through two consecutive hyperscaler mega-beats is not noise. It is the credit market systematically pricing a portfolio of all companies — not just the cloud winners — and finding that portfolio more risky than six weeks ago.

**3. USD/JPY 159.56: carry unwind is underway but hasn't hit equity yet.**

The FX trigger has fired. The equity transmission typically follows 1–3 sessions after the FX threshold breaks. Why it hasn't hit yet: AMZN +13.82% and the South Korean chip rally created a single-session counter-narrative strong enough to absorb the early carry unwinding. The dangerous scenario: when Friday's AMZN bounce fades in Monday's pre-market (the S&P opens on the weekend's news flow), and USD/JPY continues declining toward 158, the equity component of the carry unwind fires without the earnings-bounce offset.

Chip-specific risk: NVDA, TSMC, and ASML all have significant yen-funded long positioning (from the CFTC Nasdaq −74,690 extreme, now partially updated). If USD/JPY breaks 158, those longs face margin pressure independent of any fundamental news about AI chip demand.

**4. Apple supply chain: the "safe harbor" narrative fractures.**

Apple's guidance miss from supply chain snarls is the first evidence that the Iran war is disrupting beyond the Hormuz oil channel. Apple's supply chain is Asian (TSMC foundry, component assembly in Vietnam/India). The disruption mechanism: war-induced metal/component inflation + shipping route changes from the Houthi Red Sea blockade. Tim Cook's last earnings as CEO adds succession uncertainty to an already-disrupted quarter. Apple at $5T was priced as "the company that monetizes AI without spending on AI." If it can't execute on hardware because of supply chains, that premium compresses.

**5. China PMI + US GDP 1.5% = synchronized deceleration entering Q3.**

China's July PMI contraction + US Q2 GDP 1.5% (from 2.1% Q1) + UK GDP already negative = the global growth deceleration that the IMF warned about (global growth cut to 3%) is arriving in simultaneous data across all three major blocs. The Q2 export surge that propped China is unwinding. US consumer spending held (the "good" component of 1.5% GDP) — but the Q3 data will test whether consumer resilience persists into August with WTI at $85 and petrol at UK cycle highs.
